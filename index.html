Brushing Teeth (20 scripts)

  • For Brushing Teeth Without a FightI’m going to stand right here with the brush until you open.
  • For the Child Who Won’t Open Their MouthI’ll count to three, then brush for ten seconds. Only ten.
  • For When Brushing Runs Past Two MinutesOne minute on top, one minute on the bottom, then spit and rinse.
  • For the Child Who Just Says NoI’ll brush for twenty seconds, then you take the brush for twenty.
  • For the Child Who Stops Before Two MinutesFour rounds of thirty seconds, one corner of your mouth each round.
  • For the Child Who Wants to Know Why You Get to BrushYes. Watch me. Then you copy exactly what I do, on your own teeth.
  • For the Child Who Skips the RinseTwo sips of water, swish while you count to five, then spit.
  • For the Child Who Talks With the Brush InI’ll hold up two fingers when you start talking, and you keep brushing.
  • For the Child Who Hates the ToothpasteI’ll put a pea-sized dot of the mild paste on your brush and keep the mint for mine.
  • For the Child Who Walks Around While BrushingBoth feet stay on one tile square, and the timer sits on the sink where you can see it.
  • For When Brushing Collides With BedtimeThirty seconds on the back teeth, then the brush goes in the cup.
  • For the Parent Who Is Already AngryI’m putting my hand on the counter and counting five breaths before I pick up the brush.
  • For the Child Who Wants Food Right After BrushingWater, then half a banana at the table, cut in pieces, and nothing after eight.
  • For the Child Whose Brush Is Missing Every Single NightI put a hook on the wall at your height and hung a cup on it with your name on the cup.
  • For the Two Kids Whose Brushes Look Exactly the SameBlue tape on yours, red on hers, and both pieces go on tonight with your names under them.
  • For the Child Who Squeezes Half the TubeI squeeze the pea, you take the brush, and the tube goes back in the drawer.
  • For the Child Who Sees Blood and Gets ScaredSoft brush, warm water, ten slow circles on that one spot, then we move to the next.
  • For the Child Who Leaves the Brush Wet and FilthyThree seconds under the tap, thumb across the bristles, then it stands up in the cup.
  • For the Child Who Only Brushes in the MorningTonight is thirty seconds, back teeth only, with the big light on and the door open.
  • For the Morning You Have Four Minutes and No PatienceNinety seconds, the front teeth and the ones you chew with, then the brush in the cup.

Screen Time (20 scripts)

  • For When You Just Turn It Off With No WarningI hold the remote. You press the button the second the music starts.
  • For the Cry After the Screen Goes OffI’m sitting on the floor next to you until you’re done. I’m not turning it back on.
  • For the Five More Minutes That Keep GrowingI set the kitchen timer. You hear it ring, not my voice.
  • For the Child Who Sneaks the TabletWhen you want it, you ask me and I answer yes or no out loud.
  • For When You Are the One Still on the PhoneBoth phones charge on the counter from seven to eight, side by side.
  • For the Child Who Wants the Tablet at the TableThe plate, the cup, and nothing else on the table. You get up and come back as often as yo
  • For the Child Who Won’t Put It Down Before We LeaveYou finish this level, then the drawer closes, and the car leaves at eight.
  • For the Child Who Wants One More Show at BedtimeSix minutes of the show, then the tablet charges in the kitchen overnight.
  • For the Child Who Wants the Next Episode Right AwayThe credits are long enough for you to grab the remote and press pause yourself.
  • For the Child Who Says It’s Not Finished YetI write the end time on the whiteboard by the TV. It says 5:40 and it doesn’t move.
  • For Two Kids and One TabletTen minutes each, timer on the coffee table, first turn to whoever had it last.
  • For When the Weekend Limit Slips and Won’t Come BackSunday night the tablet charges in my room instead of yours. that’s the reset.
  • For the Child Who Wants the Tablet in the CarThe bag sits next to you on the back seat. You can hold it and it stays closed.
  • For the Parent Who Uses the Tablet to Get Food InI sit down across from you for the whole plate instead of standing and hovering.
  • For the Child Who Hits or Throws When It Goes OffI step one arm away from you and keep my hands open and low where you can see them.
  • For the Child Who Says I Hate You When the Screen Goes OffI take one step back and let the words land without correcting them.
  • For the Child Who Wants a Screen Because You’re on YoursI turn the screen away from you and set the phone face down on the table between us.
  • For When a Grandparent Hands Over the TabletI say the rule to her once, in front of you, in one sentence.
  • For the Child Who Loses a Game and ExplodesTwo minutes with the game paused, then you decide whether to play again or stop.
  • For When Screen Time and Homework Land at the Same HourTwenty minutes of homework at the kitchen table with the tablet face down beside it.

Mornings (20 scripts)

  • For the Child Who Won’t Wake UpI come back in five minutes with the light on and sit on the edge of the bed.
  • For the Child Who’s Awake but Won’t Get UpI put your socks on the floor by the bed and open the door so the hallway light comes in.
  • For the Child Who Won’t Get DressedShirt first, then pants, and I hand them over one at a time instead of all at once.
  • For the Child Who Picks an Outfit and Then Refuses ItI hold up both shirts and you point. The other one goes back in the drawer.
  • For the Child Who Refuses to Wash Their FaceOne wipe across the forehead, one on each cheek, then you dry with the blue towel.
  • For Morning Brushing When the Mouth Is Still AsleepTop row thirty seconds, bottom row thirty seconds, and I count out loud so you can hear it
  • For the Child Who Won’t Eat BreakfastThree bites of toast and one cup of milk, and then the plate goes in the sink.
  • For the Child Who Eats at the Speed of a SnailThe kitchen timer sits on the table with ten minutes on it, and you can watch the numbers
  • For the Child Who Can’t Get Their Shoes OnI sit on the floor and hold the shoe open while you push your foot down into it.
  • For the Child Who Needs One Specific Toy Before We GoI give you two minutes to look, and I look in the couch cushions with you.
  • For the Child Who Runs Back Inside at the DoorI hold the door open with my foot and count to three out loud.
  • For the Child Who Refuses to Go to SchoolI walk you to the gate and stand where you can see me until you’re inside.
  • For the Bathroom Trip at the Exact Moment We LeaveTwo minutes in the bathroom, and I wait by the door with the bag on my shoulder.
  • For the Morning You Are Late and They Are Still SlowI do the part that saves time: I carry your bag, and you carry your shoes.
  • For the Child Who Wants a Screen Before SchoolI put the tablet in the drawer at night so it isn’t the first thing you see.
  • For the Sibling Fight Over One Cup at BreakfastThe blue cup goes to whoever had it yesterday, so today it’s her turn.
  • For the Child Who Won’t Let You Brush Their HairI start at the ends, hold the top with my hand, and work up in small sections.
  • For the Bag That Gets Left on the Kitchen FloorThree things: bag, water bottle, folder. You say each one out loud and touch it.
  • For the Cold Morning When the Coat Stays OffI hold the coat open behind you and you push both arms in at the same time.
  • For the Morning You Already YelledI sit on the bottom step and say it once, in a normal voice, before we get in the car.
